01 · The shift

The desk stopped being the way in.

Desk bookings went from 148 a month to 19. Demand didn't die; people found the door that was open at 11 PM.

Volume fell in August and September (monsoon). The self-serve share kept climbing anyway.

02 · A day on the grid

Every cell is a state.

Paid, due, blocked, or empty with its price on it. The desk reads the whole day in one glance and knows what to do next.

03 · The flow

Three screens, not six.

Sport, date, time of day, duration. Then only the start times that are actually free. Then confirm. It runs inside WhatsApp, so there is nothing to install.

Seven in ten people who open it finish. The biggest drop is the first step, where people are just looking.

04 · When people book

Nearly one in ten books while the venue is shut.

Bookings made between 10 PM and 7 AM used to be missed calls. Now they are rows in the database.

05 · What went wrong

Two numbers I don't like.

A third of self-serve bookings get cancelled. Cancellations become credits, so the money stays; the slot often doesn't get re-sold.

On 10 October the database ran out of disk IO for 3 hours 37 minutes. The desk went back to paper.

Next: a shorter cancellation window and a waitlist, before anything new.

07 · How it was built

Two hundred days, one person.

React 19 + Vite PWA, Supabase Postgres, 12 serverless functions on Vercel, WhatsApp Flows. A separate Next.js marketing site.

68% of commits co-authored with Claude Code. I specify and review; the machine types.

08 · What I obsessed over

Five things.

  • Slot state is the product. Paid, due, blocked, empty-with-price. Every cell says what to do next.
  • Time is IST in the engine and local on the screen. No booking has landed on the wrong day.
  • Desk and bot write to one engine. A walk-in can never double-book a WhatsApp slot.
  • Three screens, not six. Every field I removed from the flow raised completion.
  • Motion is three durations and nothing else. Dark and light are the same component.

Cal.com asked for the work and what I obsessed over. This is it. The slot picker below is the same obsession, extracted into their stack.
